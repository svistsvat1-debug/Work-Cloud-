A trucker story about the night I almost quit trucking, a 2 AM phone call to my father, and what I found parked next to my truck at sunrise.
I wrote "I quit" on a napkin at the bottom of a mountain in Tennessee. Then I called the one man I was sure would be ashamed of me.

April 2003. I was 26, five years into long haul, and I hadn't been home in 36 days. I'd just missed my son's fifth birthday, and on the phone I heard him call me "the truck." That night, coming down Monteagle Mountain with 43,000 pounds of shingles, I missed a gear and watched my speedometer climb past sixty. At 2 AM, at a truck stop at the bottom, I called my father from a pay phone to tell him I was done. He listened to every word. Then he told me something, and hung up.
