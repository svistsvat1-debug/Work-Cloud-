A1. COMPETITOR DIAGNOSIS (free tools only)

- FORMULA EXTRACTION (study the whole channel, not only one video):
  1. PROOF: does the channel really get views, and are they FRESH (big views on videos from the last ~12 months)? Old success only = an old trend; say so.

  4. TITLE PATTERN: which word or structure repeats in the titles (a community, an army, a country, "How X did Y without Z", "20 things...")? This reveals the channel's real sub-niche.
  5. LENGTH PATTERN: typical video lengths.
  6. Write a FORMULA CARD: "<format> about <sub-niche>, thumbnail = <recurring element> + <text style>, title shape = <shape>, length = <range>".

B3. VISUAL STYLE — "Cinematic 2D War Animation"
FIRST open docs/video/STYLE_REFERENCE.md and every image in docs/video/style-ref/ and look at them. 03-our-approved-test-frame.jpg (built by code from test-scene.html) is the MINIMUM quality bar; every video must look at least this good. The reference stills show the target look; never copy their characters or scenes.

LOOK
- Flat 2D vector illustration with cinematic lighting. No heavy outlines; shapes are defined by color blocks.
- Volume through 2–3 tone shading, long cast shadows and rim light on edges that face the light. Soft gradients only for sky, light and glow.
- Silhouettes against a strong light source (sunset, fire, searchlight, explosion glow, window, headlights) are a core device. Tell the story through shadows and silhouettes.
- Texture on every frame: subtle paper/print texture, film grain that changes every frame, soft vignette. Never a clean "corporate vector" look.
- COLORS ARE FREE: use any colors. Choose ONE coherent palette per scene by mood, time and place (3–5 main hues + darks) and keep strong value contrast so the subject reads at a glance. Examples: dusk orange/purple for battles, night navy/teal with warm window lights, blue-white snow for the Eastern Front, ochre desert for North Africa, sepia for the home front, cold green-grey inside submarines.
- Characters: simplified proportions, minimal faces, serious tone. Prefer silhouettes, backs, hands, shadows, helmets and uniforms to carry identity. Helmets, uniforms, tanks and planes must be historically recognizable by shape, but simplified.
- Composition: cinematic 16:9, rule of thirds, 3–5 depth layers (sky, far, mid, subject, foreground framing such as wire, grass, a window frame or a doorway), plenty of negative space, one clear focal point.

MOTION (calm, slower than the reference)
- Every shot moves slowly: a push-in of 2–5% OR a lateral drift of 1–3% of the frame width, eased in and out. Layers move with parallax (the foreground moves more).
- Ambient motion that loops gently: smoke, dust, clouds, flags, searchlight sweeps, flickering fire or lamps, rain, snow, birds, water.
- Characters: minimal motion only (breathing, blink, head turn, a silhouette walking, a vehicle rolling slowly).
- No fast action, no camera shake, no hard flashes (a soft explosion glow is fine). No frozen frames, ever.

SHOT GRAMMAR (the most important rule)
- 1 SENTENCE OF VOICE ≈ 1 SHOT (4–8 s). A new shot starts on the pause between two sentences. Expect ~60–85 shots in a 6–7 min video.
- Transitions: a straight cut by default; a fade through black (0.5–0.8 s) between quests/chapters; an occasional match cut (sun -> wheel, map -> landscape).
- Each quest opens with a WIDE establishing shot, then medium and close shots, then returns to a wide shot.
- COLD OPEN: the hook plays over 2–4 strong cinematic shots, then a 2–3 s title card with the video's key phrase.

TEXT ON SCREEN (minimal)
- Location/date stamp: a small typewriter-style label in a corner ("NORMANDY · JUNE 1944"), fades in over 0.4 s.
- Quest/chapter title cards: vintage poster or stencil typography on a textured background, 2–3 s.
- Key numbers: show them INSIDE the world when possible (stenciled on a crate, a price tag, a chalkboard, a newspaper headline, a telegram); otherwise use a big clean number card in the scene's palette.
- No subtitles, no logos.

MAPS
- Stylized 2D maps in the scene palette: paper texture, muted land, darker sea, thin borders, territory fills that spread, front-line arrows moving along paths, small vehicle and flag icons (follow the WWII safety rules for symbols).

ARCHIVE FEEL WITHOUT ARCHIVE
- No real footage and no photos (rule 6). If the story needs an "archive" feel, DRAW it in our style: a film projector, a newspaper, a pinned photo, a radio, a telegram.

PERFORMANCE
- Do NOT run live SVG noise filters on every frame. Pre-render the grain and paper textures once (PNG sequence or tile), or add a mild grain in the single final ffmpeg encode. Keep the grain mild, because heavy grain inflates the file size.

REUSABLE LIBRARY (build once, then reuse)
- video-engine/components/: Shot (layers + camera move + parallax), GrainTextureVignette, AmbientFX (smoke, dust, clouds, fire flicker, rain, snow, searchlight, birds), LocationStamp, TitleCard, NumberCard, MapScene (borders, fills, arrows), FadeThroughBlack.
- video-engine/assets/: parametric WWII elements: tanks (Sherman, T-34, Panzer IV, Tiger: simplified but recognizable), planes, ships, submarines, trucks, jeeps, artillery, soldiers by army (correct helmet shapes), civilians, factories, cities and ruins, trenches, barbed wire, bunkers, landscapes per theater. Extend only when needed.

B5. BACKGROUND MUSIC (always on)
- Generated 100% free by code: compose the piece yourself (MIDI via Python, e.g. mido or pretty_midi) and render it with FluidSynth plus a free soundfont whose license allows commercial use (e.g. FluidR3_GM or GeneralUser GS; check the license before using it). No ElevenLabs, no Higgsfield, no vidIQ, no downloaded tracks.
- Style: quality cinematic WWII documentary underscore. Low strings, soft piano, distant horn/brass swells, light military snare and timpani only in tension moments. Minor keys, slow tempo (60–80 BPM). No melody that fights the voice.
- Mood map from the MUSIC MOOD tags in script_scenes.md: neutral (explanation), tension (quests, "but" moments), reveal (final boss), calm (outro). Crossfade between moods at scene boundaries.
- Mix: 25–30 dB under the voice, sidechain ducking while I speak, slight lift in pauses, fade in 2 s and fade out 4 s.
- Mix check: listen at the loudest music moment. If the music is noticeable over the voice, lower it. The voice always wins.

B6. RENDER (phone-ready master, no quality loss)
- Remotion renders a LOSSLESS MASTER first (PNG sequence or FFV1/ProRes intermediate, kept until delivery). The final video.mp4 is always ONE encode straight from that master; never re-encode an already-compressed file.
- Encode A (default, quality-based):
  ffmpeg ... -c:v libx264 -profile:v high -level 4.2 -pix_fmt yuv420p -r 30 -crf 18 -preset slow -tune animation -g 15 -bf 2 -c:a aac -b:a 320k -ar 48000 -ac 2 -movflags +faststart -tag:v avc1 video.mp4
- SIZE CAP: the final video.mp4 must be <= 400 MB. If Encode A is bigger, discard it and do Encode B from the master: 2-pass x264 at a bitrate that lands at ~390 MB:
  video_kbps = (390 * 8 * 1024 / duration_seconds) - 320   (same flags as A, but -b:v <video_kbps>k -pass 1/2 instead of -crf)
  Never go below 6000 kbps video; if the cap would force that, stop and tell me.
- VISUALLY LOSSLESS CHECK: compare video.mp4 with the master using ffmpeg's psnr and ssim filters. Pass = PSNR average >= 42 dB AND SSIM All >= 0.97. (Random film grain cannot be reproduced exactly by any codec, so SSIM tops out around 0.975 even when the difference is invisible; PSNR is the main gate.) If it fails, lower CRF / raise bitrate (within the 400 MB cap) and re-encode from the master.
- Measured on our 2D test clip: CRF 18 = PSNR 45.6 dB, SSIM 0.974, ~145 MB per 7 min; CRF 16 = ~390 MB per 7 min with no visible gain. So CRF 18 is the default sweet spot.
- Why: H.264 High + yuv420p + AAC + faststart plays and saves natively on iPhone and Android, and matches YouTube's recommended upload format (1080p30, SDR, AAC-LC 48 kHz), so nothing has to be converted on the phone.
- Voice at -14 LUFS, true peak <= -1 dBTP.
- Thumbnail: 1280x720 PNG, under 2 MB, built from the same asset library and style. Also export thumbnail.jpg (quality 95) because some phone gallery apps handle JPG better.
- Report the final file size, bitrate, and the PSNR/SSIM values. Expect roughly 130–250 MB for 6–7 min at CRF 18; grain is what inflates size, so keep it mild.
