> November 1918. A forest near Compiègne, in northern France. Inside a railway carriage, German officials sign the armistice. The First World War is over — and Germany has lost.
>
> Twenty-two years later, in June 1940, Adolf Hitler orders that same carriage pulled out of its museum and placed on the exact same spot. This time, it is France that signs.
>
> In twenty-two years, Germany went from total defeat to ruling almost all of Europe. And yet, five years after that, Hitler was dead in a bunker and Germany lay in ruins.

### КВЕСТ 1 — "The Army Germany Wasn't Allowed to Have" (1:15–3:20)

**[ЕКРАН]** «QUESTION 1/5: How do you build an army you're not allowed to have?»
**Візуал:** текст Версальського договору → парад рейхсверу → госпіталь, поранені 1918 → Гітлер 1933 → марші → Люфтваффе → німецькі війська входять у Рейнланд, натовпи вітають.

> Question one. How do you build an army when you're not allowed to have one?
>
> After 1918, the Treaty of Versailles cut Germany down. Its army was capped at one hundred thousand men. No tanks. No air force. No submarines. Germany had to accept blame for the war and pay huge reparations. Millions of Germans felt humiliated.
>
> One of them was a twenty-nine-year-old corporal. He learned of the surrender in a military hospital, temporarily blinded by gas. His name was Adolf Hitler.
>
> Fifteen years later, in January 1933, that corporal became Chancellor of Germany. Within months, he had crushed every rival party and turned Germany into a dictatorship.
>
> Then he began breaking the treaty — step by step. In 1935, he openly announced conscription and a new air force: the Luftwaffe. Britain and France protested. And did nothing.
>
> In March 1936, he made his biggest bet yet. German troops marched into the Rhineland — a border zone next to France that the treaty said must stay free of soldiers. The French army was still far stronger. If France had marched in, it would have been over.
>
> Years later, Hitler's interpreter recalled him saying: "The forty-eight hours after the march into the Rhineland were the most nerve-racking in my life. If the French had then marched in, we would have had to withdraw with our tails between our legs."
>
> France didn't march.
>
> So here's the answer to question one: Hitler built his army by betting that nobody would stop him — and winning the bet. And every time he won, he learned the same lesson: bold gambles pay off.
>
> Remember that lesson. It's our first clue.
>
> But one question remained. Why were Britain and France letting him do it?

**[ЕКРАН]** «ANSWER: He bet nobody would stop him — and won.» → «🔑 CLUE #1: EVERY GAMBLE PAYS OFF» → трекер: точка «Rhineland» загоряється.

### КВЕСТ 2 — "Why Nobody Stopped Hitler" (3:20–5:30)

**[ЕКРАН]** «QUESTION 2/5: Why did nobody stop him?»
**Візуал:** цвинтарі Першої світової → Аншлюс, натовпи у Відні → Мюнхенська конференція → Чемберлен з папером в аеропорту → танки в Празі → підписання пакту Молотова–Ріббентропа → вторгнення в Польщу.

> Question two. Why did nobody stop him?
>
> To understand this, remember what Britain and France had just lived through. The First World War had killed millions of their young men. Almost every family had lost someone. Leaders and voters were terrified of doing it again.
>
> So when Hitler made demands, they tried to give him just enough to keep the peace. This policy had a name: appeasement.
>
> In March 1938, Hitler annexed Austria. No war. Then he demanded the Sudetenland — the German-speaking border regions of Czechoslovakia. In September 1938, at a conference in Munich, Britain and France agreed to hand it over. Czechoslovakia wasn't even in the room.
>
> British Prime Minister Neville Chamberlain flew home and told cheering crowds he had brought "peace for our time."
>
> It lasted less than six months. In March 1939, Hitler took the rest of Czechoslovakia. Now it was clear: his promises meant nothing. Britain and France pledged to defend his next target — Poland.
>
> And then Hitler made a move nobody expected. In August 1939, Nazi Germany signed a pact with its sworn enemy — Stalin's Soviet Union. In public, they promised not to attack each other. In secret, they agreed to divide Eastern Europe between them.
>
> On the first of September 1939, Germany invaded Poland. Two days later, Britain and France declared war. On the seventeenth, the Soviet army invaded Poland from the east. Within weeks, Poland was gone.
>
> Answer to question two: nobody stopped Hitler because everyone feared another world war more than they feared him. By the time they understood, it was too late.
>
> But France had one of the largest armies in Europe, and Britain the largest navy in the world. Surely, this time, Hitler had gambled too far?

**[ЕКРАН]** «ANSWER: They feared war more than they feared Hitler.» → трекер: точка «Munich».

### КВЕСТ 3 — "How France Fell in Six Weeks" (5:30–8:00)

**[ЕКРАН]** «QUESTION 3/5: How did France fall in six weeks?»
**Візуал:** солдати в окопах взимку 1939–40 → форти лінії Мажино → анімована карта: удар у Бельгії, стрілки союзників на північ → танки в лісах Арденн → Седан → колони на дорогах → Ла-Манш → Черчилль → німці в Парижі → вагон у Комп'єні, 22.06.1940.

> Question three. How did France — with one of the strongest armies in the world — fall in just six weeks?
>
> For eight months after Poland, almost nothing happened in the West. Soldiers waited in trenches and forts. People called it the Phoney War.
>
> France felt safe. Along its border with Germany it had built the Maginot Line — a chain of massive concrete fortresses. Together, France and Britain had more tanks than Germany. On paper, they should have won.
>
> But the German plan didn't attack the Maginot Line. It went around it.
>
> On the tenth of May 1940, Germany attacked the Netherlands and Belgium. The best British and French troops rushed north to meet them — exactly as the Germans had hoped. Because the real blow came further south, through the Ardennes: hills and thick forest the French believed tanks could hardly cross.
>
> German panzer columns squeezed through the forest roads and broke through at Sedan. Then, instead of stopping to wait for the infantry, they raced west. By the twentieth of May — just ten days after the attack began — they reached the English Channel. The best Allied armies were cut off in the north.
>
> This speed would give German warfare its famous name: Blitzkrieg. Lightning war.
>
> On the very day the attack began, Britain got a new Prime Minister: Winston Churchill. Remember him. He's about to become the problem Hitler can't solve.
>
> By mid-June, Paris had fallen. And on the twenty-second of June 1940, Hitler took his revenge. The old railway carriage was brought back to the forest — the same carriage, the same spot — and France signed the armistice inside it.
>
> Remember that date. Twenty-second of June. It's our second clue.
>
> Answer to question three: France fell because Germany struck where France didn't expect — and moved faster than anyone thought possible.
>
> Hitler now controlled most of Western Europe. Only Britain was left. And Hitler expected it to ask for peace within weeks.
>
> It didn't. Why?

**[ЕКРАН]** «ANSWER: The blow came where no one expected — at lightning speed.» → «🔑 CLUE #2: 22 JUNE» → трекер: точка «Sedan / Compiègne».

### КВЕСТ 4 — "Why Britain Didn't Give Up" (8:00–10:30)

**[ЕКРАН]** «QUESTION 4/5: Why didn't Britain give up?»
**Візуал:** пляжі Дюнкерка, черги солдатів у воді → малі човни → Черчилль у парламенті → «Спітфайри» і «Мессершмітти» в небі → станції радара на узбережжі → оператори з картами → Лондон у вогні (Бліц) → пілоти RAF.

> Question four. Germany had beaten France in six weeks. Why didn't Britain give up?
>
> The first reason was a miracle on a beach. As German tanks closed in, hundreds of thousands of British and French soldiers were trapped around the port of Dunkirk. Then, on the twenty-fourth of May, the German tanks stopped. Hitler had approved a halt order. Historians still argue about why — but those few days gave the Allies time.
>
> Warships, ferries, fishing boats and pleasure yachts crossed the Channel again and again. By the fourth of June, around three hundred and thirty-eight thousand soldiers had been carried to England. Britain lost its equipment — but it kept its army.
>
> That same day, Churchill told Parliament: "We shall fight on the beaches… we shall never surrender."
>
> The second reason was in the sky. To invade Britain, Germany first needed control of the air. In the summer of 1940, the Luftwaffe threw its bombers and fighters at southern England. This was the Battle of Britain.
>
> But Britain had a secret weapon: radar. A chain of stations along the coast could see German planes coming and send British fighters exactly where they were needed. German pilots kept running into fighters that seemed to be waiting for them.
>
> Then, in September, Germany switched from attacking RAF airfields to bombing London. It was a nightmare for Londoners — but it gave the RAF the breathing space it needed.
>
> On the seventeenth of September 1940, Hitler postponed the invasion of Britain. It never came.
>
> Churchill said of the pilots: "Never in the field of human conflict was so much owed by so many to so few."
>
> Answer to question four: Britain didn't give up because it saved its army at Dunkirk, won the battle in the sky, and had a leader who refused to make peace.
>
> And for the first time, Hitler's gamble had failed. That's our third clue.
>
> He was stuck in a war he couldn't end. So he did what he always did. He raised the stakes.

**[ЕКРАН]** «ANSWER: Dunkirk. Radar. Churchill.» → «🔑 CLUE #3: THE FIRST LOST GAMBLE» → трекер: точка «London».

### КВЕСТ 5 — "Operation Barbarossa" (10:30–12:50)

**[ЕКРАН]** «QUESTION 5/5: Why attack the Soviet Union while Britain was still fighting?»
**Візуал:** Гітлер над картою → колони на кордоні на світанку → анімована карта: три стрілки вглиб СРСР → полонені → Київ 1941 → евакуація заводів на схід → багнюка, техніка застрягла → сніг, замерзлі солдати → контрнаступ під Москвою → газетні заголовки 11.12.1941.

> Question five. Why would Hitler attack the Soviet Union — while Britain was still fighting?
>
> For Hitler, the war in the East was never just strategy. For years he had written and spoken about seizing "living space" in Eastern Europe, and about destroying what he called "Jewish Bolshevism." The pact with Stalin was only ever temporary.
>
> But there was also a cold calculation. Hitler believed Britain was holding on because it hoped the Soviet Union, and later America, would join the war. Crush the Soviet Union quickly, he reasoned, and Britain would have no hope left.
>
> After Poland and France, he — and many of his generals — believed the Soviet Union would fall just as fast. A few months. Before winter.
>
> On the twenty-second of June 1941 — exactly one year to the day after France signed in that railway carriage — more than three million German soldiers crossed into the Soviet Union. Operation Barbarossa.
>
> At first, it looked like another lightning victory. Whole Soviet armies were surrounded. In September, Kyiv fell, and hundreds of thousands of Soviet soldiers were taken prisoner.
>
> But the Soviet Union didn't collapse. It was enormous. Every time one army was destroyed, another appeared. Factories were moved east, beyond the reach of German bombers. Then came the rain, the mud — and the winter. German soldiers had no proper winter clothing, because no one had planned to still be fighting.
>
> By December, German troops were only a few dozen kilometres from Moscow. Then, on the fifth of December 1941, the Soviet army struck back. For the first time, the German army was thrown into retreat.
>
> Six days later, Hitler declared war on the United States.
>
> Answer to question five: Hitler attacked the Soviet Union because he believed one more lightning victory would end the war. Instead, he now faced the British Empire, the Soviet Union and the United States — all at once.

**[ЕКРАН]** «ANSWER: One more lightning victory — that never came.» → трекер: точка «Moscow».

### ★ ФІНАЛ — "The Day Hitler Lost World War 2" (12:50–13:50)

**Візуал:** три ключі 🔑 з'являються на екрані й з'єднуються → дата «22 JUNE 1941» → бункер → руїни Берліна → прапор над Рейхстагом → порожня галявина в Комп'єні.

> So — when did Hitler lose the Second World War?
>
> Not in 1945, in the ruins of Berlin. Not even at Stalingrad.
>
> Look at our three clues. One: every gamble paid off — the Rhineland, Austria, Czechoslovakia, France. Two: the twenty-second of June — his greatest triumph, in that railway carriage. Three: Britain — the first gamble he lost.
>
> And when that gamble failed, he made the biggest bet of all.
>
> Hitler lost the war on the twenty-second of June 1941 — one year to the day after his triumph in the carriage. The victory that made him master of Europe is exactly what convinced him he could not lose.
>
> Germany could win short wars. It could never win a long war against the three greatest powers on earth.
>
> On the thirtieth of April 1945, Hitler took his own life in his bunker in Berlin. Eight days later, Germany surrendered.
>
> And the railway carriage? It was taken to Germany as a trophy. In 1945, it was destroyed.
>
> Hitler's war ended the way his story began: in defeat.

- **Музика:** оркестровий ембієнт, −20…−25 dB під голосом. Наростання — у кульмінації кожного квесту.
