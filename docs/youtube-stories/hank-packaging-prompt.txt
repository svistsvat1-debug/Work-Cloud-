STYLE 1 - ROAD MEMOIR (like "Clyde Barrett", 965K views in 3 weeks)
 Use when the strongest moment is a road, weather or truck scene: fog, storm, snow, night highway, bridge, a truck following, a hill, a ravine, something seen through the windshield.

 When the mystery is a place or an object seen from the road (a house, a light, a sign, a figure), show it from the driver's seat: a first-person view through the windshield, the top of the steering wheel and the dashboard in the foreground, the key object large and sharp outside. STYLE 3 - THE MOMENT (like "Onyx Heart Stories" x33, "True Tale Time" x14)

 Look: one hyperreal cinematic scene at the tensest moment, an older main character (or the key object itself) in the center, the key object marked with one thin red circle or one red arrow. No text, or at most 2-3 words when the scene alone does not show the paradox.

STEP 3. RULES FOR EVERY THUMBNAIL
=====================================================================
- It looks like the video: same places, objects, mood and colors as the story; the scene really happens in the video, ideally in the first 60 seconds.
- Adult, serious, cinematic look for men 50-65+: no cartoon, no neon, no emojis, no childish faces, no clutter.
- Maximum 3 main elements (text counts as one).
- CLARITY: the key object of the mystery (the house, the light, the letter, the seal...) is big and sharp, close to the camera, at least one third of the frame. Never a small detail far in the background. It must read in half a second on a phone.
- ONE FOCUS: one point where the eye lands first, the key object or Hank's face. The brightest, warmest spot in the frame is the key object. Remove everything that competes with it: extra cars, signs, rain streaks over the object, busy sky, clutter. One thin red circle or one red arrow may mark the key object (any style except Style 2).
- HOOK TEXT: 1-4 words (Style 2 is the only exception), huge, all capitals, hitting the main paradox from a side the title does not show. Never repeat the title or its key fact: if the title already has the year, the text uses another fact (a count, a distance, a rule like "ONLY FOR TRUCKS").
- TRUTH: every word on the thumbnail is literally true in the script. If the light comes on for every truck, never write "ONLY FOR ME".
- TITLE FIT: the title says the paradox in words, the thumbnail shows it in a picture, from a personal angle (Hank's face, or his view from the cab), never as a generic road landscape.
- PHONE TEST (silently, before you reply): imagine the thumbnail 160 pixels wide. If the key object and the text are not clear in one second, make the object bigger, cut details and shorten the text.
- Faces: big, clear, well lit, exaggerated but believable emotion (wide eyes, raised eyebrows, open mouth), looking at the camera or at the key object.
- Strong contrast: cold blue night and fog against one warm yellow light; slightly more saturated than the video.
- The bottom-right corner stays empty and dark (YouTube shows the video length there).
- Format 16:9, 1280x720.
- Honest: everything the thumbnail promises is in the video. Never "TRUE STORY", never bodycam or "REC" frames, never fake news style.
