STEP 2. CHOOSE THE STYLE (what works in HFY right now, October 2026)
=====================================================================
Two families of titles dominate fresh HFY uploads:
- War and deathworld: "Aliens Called Humans Fools And Landed 60,000 Troops—The Deathworld Sent Them FLEEING!", "Aliens Called Humans Weak—Until a Dangerous Human Warship Destroyed Their Fleet!", "Alien Warns: Do Not Attack the Humans—They Will Reduce Your Empire to Dust", "Alien Medics Declared Him Broken—Then the Deathworlder Rose and Asked for His Rifle".
- Humble human and alien royalty: "The Human Janitor Shared His Last Meal — Never Knowing the 'Maid' Was an Alien Queen", "The Human Mechanic Fixed Alien Kids' Toys for Free—Unaware They Were Royal Heirs", "The Alien Woman Tried to Leave Quietly — But the Human Mechanic Saw Her Little Girl Staring at His...".

STYLE 1 - THE STANDOFF (war, deathworld, sleeping giant, last stand, protective fury)
 Use when the story is a confrontation: humans against an army, a fleet, a warlord, a court.
 Look: one human in the foreground, seen from behind or in three-quarter view, small but calm and steady; facing him a huge alien army, warlord or fleet filling the frame. Strong scale contrast. A huge 2-4 word phrase in the top part of the frame: first line pure white, second line bright yellow ("THEY CALLED US WEAK", "WRONG PLANET").

STYLE 2 - THE ROYAL MOMENT (untouchable tamed, hidden depth, humble human, alien royalty, PG romance)
 Use when the hook is a simple human act that touches a powerful alien: sharing food, fixing a toy, a hug, a song.
 Look: one intimate cinematic scene at the tensest or warmest moment. LEFT: the alien royal (empress, queen, heir) with a strong, readable emotion (shock, tears, disbelief). RIGHT: the humble human doing the simple act, the key object (bowl of food, toy, tool) large and sharp between them, marked with one thin red circle or one red arrow. No text, or at most 2-3 words.

STYLE 3 - ALIEN REACTION (galactic question, biology or culture shock, wrong classification, trial)
 Use when the hook is something about humans that aliens cannot understand.
 Look: a big close-up of one alien face with an exaggerated but believable reaction (wide eyes, open mouth, raised crest), looking at the human thing that shocks it, which fills the other side of the frame (a cup of coffee, a sleeping human, a dog, tears, a hot pepper, a medical scan). Text 2-4 words framed as the alien's thought ("THEY DON'T SLEEP?", "IT'S A PET?").

STYLE 4 - THE FLEET ARRIVES (sleeping giant, humanity answers)
 Use only when the climax is humanity's mass answer and that answer appears in the video.
 Look: a dark sky or orbit; human warships emerging in a long line, the leading ship big and sharp, alien ships small in the foreground. One short line of text in white and yellow.

Pick the best style for THUMBNAIL A and the second-best different style for THUMBNAIL B.

STEP 3. RULES FOR EVERY THUMBNAIL
=====================================================================
- It looks like the video: the same species, ships, places, objects and colors as the story (use VIDEO LOOK if given); the scene really happens in the video, ideally in the first 60 seconds.
- Adult, cinematic, serious sci-fi look: photorealistic, no cartoon, no clip-art green aliens, no blurry flying saucers, no emojis, no clutter.
- The alien looks exactly like the species in the script: its body, skin, eyes, clothing and size. Never a generic grey alien unless the script says so.
- Maximum 3 main elements (text counts as one).
- CLARITY: the key face or object is big and sharp, close to the camera, at least one third of the frame. It must read in half a second on a phone.
- ONE FOCUS: one point where the eye lands first. The brightest spot in the frame is the key face or object. Remove everything that competes with it. One thin red circle or one red arrow may mark the key object.
- HOOK TEXT: 1-4 words, huge, all capitals, hitting the conflict from a side the title does not show. Never repeat the title or its key fact.
- TRUTH: every word and every scene on the thumbnail is literally in the script. Never promise a battle that does not happen or a romance that is not there.
- TITLE FIT: the title tells the conflict in words, the thumbnail shows it in a picture; together they make one question.
- PHONE TEST (silently, before you reply): imagine the thumbnail 160 pixels wide. If the key face or object and the text are not clear in one second, make them bigger, cut details and shorten the text.
- Faces (human and alien): big, clear, well lit, exaggerated but believable emotion, looking at the camera or at the key object.
- Strong contrast: deep space blue and black against one warm orange or golden light on the key face or object; slightly more saturated than the video.
- The bottom-right corner stays empty and dark (YouTube shows the video length there).
- Format 16:9, 1280x720.
- Honest: everything the thumbnail promises is in the video. Never "TRUE STORY", never fake news style, never real logos, flags, uniforms, real people or franchise characters.

FONTS AND COLORS (always the same, the channel style):
- Main text: extra bold condensed sans-serif poster font in the style of Anton or Bebas Neue, all capitals, tight spacing, thick black outline, strong soft black drop shadow.
- Colors: pure white #FFFFFF and bright yellow #FFD400. Red #E10600 only for one thin circle or one arrow on the key object.

STEP 4. HOW TO WRITE EACH THUMBNAIL PROMPT
=====================================================================
Write one complete paragraph that an image generator can use directly. It must contain:
1. "Cinematic photorealistic sci-fi YouTube thumbnail, 16:9, 1280x720," and the style name.
2. The scene: place (ship bridge, throne hall, station corridor, alien planet...), light, the key face or object (large, close, at least one third of the frame, the brightest spot), where everything sits in the frame, and what is left out.
3. The characters: the alien's full look from the script (body, skin, eyes, clothing, size) and the human's look (age, build, work clothes, one signature object), their emotions and where they look.
4. TEXT (if any): the exact words in quotes, "render exactly, spelled correctly", line by line, with the color of each line, the font description from above, the size and the exact position, and "not covering the faces".
5. Colors and grade: deep space blue and black, one warm orange or golden light on the key point, slightly saturated cinematic teal-and-orange grade.
6. "Bottom-right corner empty and dark. No other text, no logos, no watermarks, no borders."

STEP 5. RULES FOR THE TITLE
=====================================================================
- English, 60-90 characters with spaces (count them). The conflict and both key roles must be in the first 50 characters, because phones cut long titles.
- Built like what works in HFY right now: two parts joined by an em dash. Part one: the aliens' wrong judgment or the human's humble act. Part two: "Until...", "Then...", "Never Knowing...", "Unaware...", "But..." that opens the question.
- Specific role nouns (Janitor, Mechanic, Medic, Empress, Admiral, Warlord) and at least one exact number when the script has one ("60,000 Troops", "8 Alien Blades").
- Opens a question that is answered only near the end. Never reveal the ending or the twist.
- Numbers as digits. At most one word in capitals. No emojis, no hashtags. Do not add "HFY" or "Sci-Fi Story" to the title; they go in the description and tags.
- If TRENDING TITLES are given, match their rhythm and wording style, never their story.
- Does not repeat the thumbnail text; together they make one question.
- Everything it promises is in the video, ideally in its first 60 seconds.
