STEP 4 — THE FIRST MINUTE (most important part of the video)
The first 150–165 spoken words (0:00–1:00) decide whether the viewer stays. Build them exactly in this order:
- 0:00–0:05 (≈12 words) — Cold open: the single most visual, highest-stakes or strangest moment of the story, usually taken from the climax. No names yet, no context, no greeting.
- 0:05–0:15 (≈25 words) — The title promise, stated plainly: who the human is, who the alien is, what just went wrong. The viewer must recognize the title here.
- 0:15–0:30 (≈40 words) — Stakes and the aliens' wrong belief about humans; plant the human's habit or object without explaining it.
- 0:30–0:45 (≈40 words) — A first mini-reveal or escalation that raises a sharper question.
- 0:45–1:00 (≈40 words) — Open loop and transition into the story ("...but to understand why the Admiral bowed, you have to see what happened nine days earlier."). Never answer the cold open here.
- No channel intro, no "subscribe", no lore dump, no slow description in this minute. Every sentence must add new information.
- The first minute works like a trailer. From 1:00 the story starts at its real beginning and runs in order; the cold-open moment is reached again near the climax and only then fully explained.

STEP 5 — STORY (about 20 minutes)
- Length: 3,000–3,300 words in total, including the first minute. Budget after the first minute: setup ≈550, escalation ≈800, complications and dark moment ≈900, climax ≈550, resolution ≈200. Write every scene in full; never summarize or rush the ending.
- Arc: the aliens' wrong belief about humans → escalation → midpoint reversal → dark moment where the human trait seems to fail → climax where that trait, planted early, wins and costs something → resolution with a final image that mirrors the cold open and a quotable last line.
- Retention: every 400–600 words add a new question, reveal, escalation or reversal; keep at least two open questions alive until the final act; include a genuinely funny moment at least every 1,000 words. Place a strong re-hook (a reveal plus a new question) near 25%, 50% and 75% of the story.
- Write for the screen: in every scene name one concrete visible action, object or place that can be shown; avoid scenes that are only inner thoughts.
- Aliens are intelligent and competent, with coherent motives; vary their reactions (not always "shocked" or "terrified"). Humans win through a specific trait, never luck or effortless superiority.
- At most 4 named characters and 5 invented terms; explain each term in the sentence where it first appears; invented terms are at most 3 syllables and easy for text-to-speech. Keep names, ranks, anatomy and timeline consistent.
- Real-world facts about humans must be true; exaggerations are framed as alien beliefs.
- No moral lecture and no narrator praising humanity — events prove it.

STEP 6 — VOICEOVER AND SUBTITLE FORMAT (for the narration text)
- Only words meant to be spoken: no headings, chapter titles, brackets, stage directions, sound cues, asterisks, emojis, lists or ALL CAPS.
- Numbers as words; no abbreviations, units or acronyms.
- Name the speaker at every speaker change. Aliens speak formally without contractions; humans speak casually, with idioms and understatement.
- Paragraphs of 2–5 sentences; vary sentence length; no sentence longer than 25 words (subtitles must stay readable); in the first minute keep most sentences under 12 words. A blank line marks a scene change.
- Never use: "little did they know", "a testament to", "in a world where", "the silence was deafening", "a chill ran down", "time seemed to stand still", "couldn't help but", "unwavering", "palpable", "tapestry", "delve", "beacon of hope", "the weight of", "something shifted", "let out a breath", "in that moment", "everything changed", "sent shockwaves", "would never be the same", "eyes widened". At most once each: "space orcs", "death world", "humans are insane", "what are you". Never repeat a metaphor or image.

STEP 7 — VISUALS FOR GOOGLE FLOW
- Every visual prompt is in English, 25–60 words: subject + one clear action + setting + camera (shot size and movement) + lighting, then the STYLE SUFFIX. One action per clip. No text, letters, subtitles, logos or watermarks in frame. No dialogue and no lip movement (the narration is a voiceover). Aspect ratio 16:9.
- Consistency: in every prompt, copy the character and location descriptions from the VISUAL BIBLE word for word. Never describe a character differently.
- Type of shot: VIDEO = a Flow/Veo clip (about 8 seconds, trimmed in editing); STILL = an image animated in editing with a slow zoom or pan. Use VIDEO for action, reveals, the first minute and the climax; use STILL for calm narration.
- Editing rhythm: first minute — a new shot every 2–3 seconds (20–25 shots, mostly VIDEO); minutes 1–5 — every 4–6 seconds; after that — every 8–12 seconds, reusing establishing shots; return to a 2–3 second rhythm at each re-hook and at the climax.
- Safety for visuals: no gore, no realistic weapons pointed at people, no real places, logos, brands, uniforms or flags of real nations, no real people.
