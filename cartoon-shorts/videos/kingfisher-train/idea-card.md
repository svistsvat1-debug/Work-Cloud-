# Approved idea card - kingfisher-train (approved 2026-11-13: "Робимо")

Source: found by Claude (MODE B), owner's theme "birds + physics" (the first card, eagles on power lines, was rejected: "this idea already existed").

## Voiceover (approved, used word for word)
Why did Japan give its bullet train a nose like a bird's beak? Its old nose shoved air into tunnels so hard the tunnels went boom. But a kingfisher dives into water with barely a splash. So engineers copied its beak, and the train slipped in quietly.

47 words, 250 characters, estimated ~18.1 s (real take: 15.6 s).

Connector chain: why a beak? -> (because) the old nose shoved air into tunnels -> boom -> but a kingfisher dives with barely a splash -> so engineers copied the beak -> the train slipped in quietly.

## Packaging
Titles: "Why does Japan's bullet train have a bird's beak?" (pick) / "Why did Japan copy a bird to fix its fastest train?" / "What happens when a bullet train enters a tunnel?". Cover text: TUNNELS WENT BOOM.

## Sources
- Trellis / GreenBiz: tunnel boom heard by residents ~400 m away; kingfisher goes from air into water ~800x denser; 500 Series ~30 % less air resistance, ~13 % less power than the 300 series; in service 22 Mar 1997.
- Gigazine (2017), Interesting Engineering, Medium "Nakatsu's Kingfisher".
- No numbers in the voiceover on purpose (the popular "10 % faster, 15 % less electricity" figures vary by source).

Backups: No.2 flamingos on one leg (Chang & Ting 2017), No.3 silent owl flight.
