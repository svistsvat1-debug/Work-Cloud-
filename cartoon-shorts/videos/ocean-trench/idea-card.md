# Idea card (approved 2026-11-11) - ocean-trench

Source: found by Claude (MODE B), second card (the first idea, "nuke a hurricane", had already been made).

Idea: a guy dumps a trash bag into the deepest ocean trench so the Earth "eats" it; the seafloor moves about as fast as
fingernails grow, and part of what sinks can come back up through a volcano.

Voiceover (approved, 69 words, 363 characters):
> Why don't we just dump our trash into the deepest ocean trench? The seafloor there slides into the Earth, so it eats everything, right? But it slides about as fast as your fingernails grow. So one trash bag takes millions of years to go down. Even then, some of what sinks ends up in magma, which means a bit of your trash could come back up... through a volcano.

Connector chain: hook -> (so) obvious answer, right? -> but fingernail speed -> so millions of years -> (but) even then magma, which means -> back through a volcano (payoff, loops).

Why it should work: the "where do we put the trash" question is proven (Zack D. Films volcano 16.7M, Danno Cal Drawings 1.6M, trash into space 31.9M); the trench version is almost unmade (845-view Q&A); own angle: the trash returns through a volcano.

Packaging: titles "Why don't we dump trash into the deepest ocean trench?" (pick) / "What if we threw trash into the Mariana Trench?" / "Can the ocean floor eat our trash?"; cover text "Fingernail speed?".

Sources: USGS plate-speed FAQ; USGS Yellowstone FAQ (fingernails ~3.6 cm/yr); USGS "Subduction zones: not relevant to present-day problems of waste disposal"; Morris, Leeman & Tera, Nature 1990 (Be-10 in arc lavas); Mariana plate rates ~2-10 cm/yr (Wikipedia, secondary).
Caveat: the fingernail comparison is the USGS general statement; the northern Mariana trench moves 2-3x faster, hence "about".

Backups: #2 bury trash in Antarctic ice (ice flows to the sea); #3 catch lightning for electricity.
