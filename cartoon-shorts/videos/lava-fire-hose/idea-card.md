# Idea card (approved 2026-11-11) - lava-fire-hose

Source: found by Claude (MODE B). Approved by the owner ("Роби тема супер").

Idea: why don't we just stop lava with a fire hose? A hose failed in Hawaii (too much lava), but in 1973 Iceland pumped
seawater onto a lava flow for five months, the front froze into a wall, and that lava now shelters the harbour from storms.

Voiceover (approved, 69 words, 371 characters):
> Why don't we just stop lava with a fire hose? Water turns lava into rock, right? But firefighters in Hawaii tried it, and the lava kept coming. There was too much lava. So when lava crept toward Iceland's top fishing port, they pumped the ocean onto it. For five months. The front froze into a wall, so the lava stopped. And that wall still protects the port from storms.

Sources: USGS GIP "Man Against Volcano" (https://pubs.usgs.gov/gip/heimaey/heimaey.pdf): Iceland's most important
fishing port; lava 1,030-1,080 C; 6 Feb - 10 Jul 1973 about 8 million cubic yards (~6 million m3) of seawater pumped; the
tongue that almost blocked the harbour now acts as a breakwater protecting it from storms. USGS OF 97-724
(https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1997/of97-724/edintro.html): Hawaii Nov 1986 firemen's spraying "failed because of the volume
of lava"; Heimaey cooling called a success. USGS OF 97-724 methods (https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1997/of97-724/32html/methods.html):
the cooled tongue "did not move from that time on". Oregon State Volcano World
(https://volcano.oregonstate.edu/news/volcano-vs-man-tale-eldfell): 43 pumps, 30+ km of pipe.
Caveat: the eruption was also weakening, so "so the lava stopped" describes what happened without claiming water alone did it.
