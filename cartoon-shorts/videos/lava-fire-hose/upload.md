## Description (copy as is)
Water turns lava into rock, so why not just hose it down? Firefighters in Hawaii tried in 1986, but there was too much lava. In 1973, when lava threatened Iceland's most important fishing port, crews pumped seawater onto it for five months. The front froze, the lava stopped, and that lava now shields the harbor from storms.

## Fact notes (in case a commenter asks)
- "Firefighters in Hawaii tried it": USGS OF 97-724 - in November 1986 local firemen sprayed municipal water on the Kilauea
  flow front near Kalapana to save one home; "the attempt failed because of the volume of lava".
- "Iceland's top fishing port": USGS calls Vestmannaeyjar (Heimaey) "Iceland's most important fishing port"; the harbour is
  "the best along the entire south coast of Iceland".
- "Five months": the cooling ran from 6-7 February to 10 July 1973 (USGS).
- "Pumped the ocean onto it": about 8 million cubic yards (~6 million m3, ~6.2 million tonnes) of seawater; 43 pumps and
  more than 30 km of pipe (USGS; Oregon State Volcano World).
- "The front froze into a wall": the cooled tongue near the harbour entrance "did not move from that time on" (USGS OF 97-724).
  The eruption was also weakening, so the video says what happened, not that water alone stopped the volcano.
- "Protects the port from storms": USGS - "The tongue of lava that almost blocked the harbor entrance ... is now acting as a
  breakwater, helping to protect the harbor from storms."
- Lava temperature on Heimaey: 1,030-1,080 C (USGS).
