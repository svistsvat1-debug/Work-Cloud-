## Description (copy as is)

## Fact notes (in case a commenter asks)
- "In 1960, scientists calculated the date of doomsday": Heinz von Foerster, Patricia M. Mora and Lawrence W. Amiot
  (University of Illinois), Science, 4 November 1960, "Doomsday: Friday, 13 November, A.D. 2026". Subtitle: "At this date
  human population will approach infinity if it grows as it has grown in the last two millenia."
- "Infinite people": their formula N = 1.79e11 / (2026.87 - t)^0.99 goes to infinity at t = 2026.87 (the chart in the video
  is drawn from this formula).
- "We'd all get squeezed to death. Their words.": "our great-great-grandchildren will not starve to death. They will be
  squeezed to death."
- "For decades, real numbers kept up with the curve": the formula beat most demographers' projections through the 1970s
  and 1980s and began to fall behind around 1993 (The Conversation); a 2010 analysis found the population still ahead of
  schedule in 1994. The yellow dots are rounded UN estimates (1970 3.7 bn, 1980 4.4 bn, 1990 5.3 bn).
- "Families shrank to just over two kids": UN WPP 2024 - global fertility 2.25 live births per woman (down from 3.31 in 1990).
- "That was their cure for doomsday": the paper says "cutting the birth rate to about half its present value or, in other
  words, cutting the size of an average family to just a little above two children".
- Many demographers read the paper as half a joke at the time (letters to Science, 1961); the video says "their math
  said", not that it was a prophecy.
