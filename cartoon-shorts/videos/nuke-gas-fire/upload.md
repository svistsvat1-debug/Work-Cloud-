## Description
In 1963 a gas well in Uzbekistan blew out and burned for almost three years. In 1966 Soviet engineers set off a nuclear charge about 1.5 km underground right beside it: the blast squeezed the well shut, and the flame went out 23 seconds later.

Sources:
- M. D. Nordyke, "The Soviet Program for Peaceful Uses of Nuclear Explosions", Science & Global Security 7:1 (1998), p. 35: https://scienceandglobalsecurity.org/archive/sgs07nordyke.pdf
- OSTI record of the same report (Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory): https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/514386
- Urtabulak gas field: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urtabulak_gas_field

## Notes for the upload
- Cartoon exaggeration (the cork, the faces, the marshmallow, the garden hose) is for the characters only; the voice-over facts follow the sources above.
- Real details kept out of the 25 s voice-over: well No. 11 lost control on 1 Dec 1963 at 2,450 m; more than 12 million m3 of gas a day; capping attempts pushed gas into nearby wells; a 30 kt charge in a slant hole about 35 m from the well at ~1,500 m; detonated 30 Sep 1966.
