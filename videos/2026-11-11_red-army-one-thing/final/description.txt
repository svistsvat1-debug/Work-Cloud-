Every day, a Red Army soldier did dozens of jobs just to stay alive. But there was one tiny job, ten seconds with a pencil, that many of them refused to do. More than eighty years later, people are still searching the forests because of that choice.

We live through one ordinary day of a Soviet front-line soldier in 1943: the dugout, the foot wraps, breakfast in the dark, the spade, the winter, American trucks and "second front" tins, the 100 grams, triangle letters home, Order No. 227, and the small black capsule he carried around his neck.

The Red Army was a multinational army: its soldiers came from Ukraine, Kazakhstan, Georgia, Belarus, Russia and every other Soviet republic. This video is educational history about ordinary people. It does not glorify any regime or army.

Main sources and further reading
- Order No. 138 of 15 March 1941 on personal loss records and medallions (Rossiyskaya Gazeta, 2020)
- State Defense Committee decree No. 562, 22 August 1941 (the "100 grams")
- Order No. 227, 28 July 1942 ("Not one step back")
- Catherine Merridale, "Ivan's War: Life and Death in the Red Army, 1939-1945"
- Reports on volunteer search teams (AFP, Meduza)
Some figures (trench length at Kursk, Lend-Lease vehicle totals, the number of missing) differ between sources, so the video uses rounded wording.
