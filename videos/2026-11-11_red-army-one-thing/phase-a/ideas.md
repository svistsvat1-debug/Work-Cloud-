# A2 — 3 idea options

There are no previous videos of ours yet (`docs/video/competitors.md` does not exist), so the "continue our winner" rule does not apply this time.

---

## Option 1 — ⭐ RECOMMENDED: "One ordinary day of a Red Army soldier"
- **Premise:** we live through one ordinary day of a Soviet front-line soldier in 1943, job by job. The final reveal is the one ten-second job most of them refused to do: filling in the slip in their identity capsule.
- **Formula:** FORMULA TRANSFER (Ink Story's "What did soldiers actually do all day").
- **Swap axis:** country/army, US → Soviet. Same axis Legacy of Steel used for US → German.
- **Sub-niche check: GREEN**
  - Formula proof: Ink Story, 9.76k subs, "What Did WW2 Soldiers Actually Do All Day?", 882k, 33x, 2026. Legacy of Steel, **598 subs**, "What Did German WW2 Soldiers Actually Do All Day?", **150k, 155x**.
  - Subject demand: Squire "Every Soviet Soldier in 1942" 480k · Tasting History "Cooking on the Soviet Home Front" 2.56M · Ink Story "Your Life as Every Rank in the Soviet Red Army" 71k · Ink Story "POV: You're A Soviet Soldier" 61k.
  - Exact formula + subject: `ytsearch30:"what did soviet soldiers actually do all day ww2"` found **no** video with this angle. Results are battles, Barbarossa, "What Did Stalin Do All Day" (275 views).
- **Why people will click:** the audience of the 882k video and its follow-ups has already proven it wants this. The Soviet soldier is the army they know least about, and it has unusual details: foot wraps, the 100 grams, triangle letters, and the identity capsule.
- **Why it is NOT a copy:** a different army, a different structure (one day split into quests, ending in a mystery reveal rather than a description), a different title shape (not "What Did … Actually Do All Day"), and our own marker-whiteboard style.
- ⚠️ **Note for you:** the subject is the Soviet army. We tell it purely as human history: the soldier is shown as multinational (Ukrainian, Kazakh, Georgian…), there is **no Soviet symbolism** (no star, no hammer and sickle) on screen or in the thumbnail, and the regime is not glorified (Order 227 and the penal units are told factually). If you'd rather not make this subject at all, pick option 2.

## Option 2: "How many men stood behind one rifleman?"
- **Premise:** for every soldier who fought on the front line, how many people supported him: drivers, cooks, mail clerks, mechanics? The answer is a single number, revealed in the final block.
- **Formula:** NEXT EPISODE of Ink Story's 882k video. The viewer has just learned that soldiers rarely fought, and the natural next question is "who did all the work?"
- **Swap axis:** front line → logistics ("the tail").
- **Sub-niche check: YELLOW**
  - The only video in this formula is WARBOARD "How Many Men Stood Behind ONE WW2 Soldier? Not 2 or 3". It is brand new, with 0 views and a 2-video channel, so it proves nothing either way.
  - Adjacent: Euro Defence "How Military Logistics Actually Works" 464k · Mark Felton "How Much Did WW2 Tanks Cost?" 526k.
- **Why people will click:** a big number in a whiteboard format, and logistics explained simply is rare.
- **Why it is NOT a copy:** a different subject (support, not the frontline soldier's day) and a different question.
- Risk: demand for this specific question is unproven, and the key number varies by army and source, so it needs careful sourcing.

## Option 3: "Every home front job in wartime Britain, ranked by danger"
- **Premise:** land girl, munitions worker, air-raid warden, firefighter, merchant seaman… which civilian job was the most dangerous?
- **Formula:** FORMULA TRANSFER (Inside the Steel's "ranked from safest to deadliest").
- **Swap axis:** war machine → home front (civilians).
- **Sub-niche check: YELLOW with a RED edge**
  - The home front version is empty: "What If You Had to Survive the Deadliest Jobs of the London Blitz?" has 10 views.
  - The general "worst WW2 jobs" topic is owned by big channels: BE AMAZED "The Worst Roles You Could Be Assigned In WW2" 7.16M, The Infographics Show "Jobs That Would Get You Killed During WWII" 520k.
  - The "ranked safest → deadliest" format is overrun with copycats that get almost no views.
- **Why people will click:** "civilians at war" is a surprising angle, and the ranking creates natural suspense.
- **Why it is NOT a copy:** a different subject (civilians, not crews).
- Risk: big channels own the generic topic, and the title shape is overused.

---

## Recommendation
**Option 1.** It is the only option with a GREEN check: the formula was proven twice in 2026 (882k and 150k on tiny channels), and its subject is still empty. It suits whiteboard well (a timeline of one day, map, icons), and the final boss (the capsule) gives a strong emotional payoff.
