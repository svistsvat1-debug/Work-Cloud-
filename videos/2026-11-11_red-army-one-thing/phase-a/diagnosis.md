## 2. Competitor #1 — Ink Story (the formula we transfer)

- Channel: Ink Story (@Ink-Story-2), 9.76k subs, 36 videos, median ~27k.
- Video: "What Did WW2 Soldiers Actually Do All Day?" — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z4DB1tppFU8 — 882k views, 17 min.
- Age: it sits between videos dated 2026-07-25 and 2026-11-02 in the channel's newest-first list, so it is **under 4 months old**. The follow-up "…All Night?" (2026-11-02) has 51k in 9 days.
- PROOF (fresh?): **yes**. The channel's top 5 (882k, 553k, 452k, 377k, 231k) all come from 2026.

**TOP-10 thumbnail pattern** (contact sheet in the scratchpad, not committed):
- Every thumbnail has **one big cartoon soldier with a white "meme" face** and an exaggerated emotion: fear (wide eyes, sweat), smug, or relaxed.
- 4 of 10 have **1–3 words of yellow block capitals** that contradict what you expect: "NO FIGHTING", "30 SECONDS LEFT", "WRONG WAR?", "YOUR EMPIRE IS FALLING".
- The "Every Rank" videos use a split: the miserable private on the left, the smug general on the right (a contrast tactic).
- The background is the soldier's world (trench, landing craft, palace).
- **Click tactic:** an emotional face plus a short line that breaks expectations.

**Title pattern:** "What Did [ordinary group] Actually Do All [Day/Night]?" · "POV: You Are a [nation] Soldier in WW2" · "Your Life as Every Rank in the [army]". The real sub-niche is **the life of an ordinary soldier from one specific army**. The army is the axis they rotate.

**Length pattern:** 16–20 min (median 18).

> **FORMULA CARD (Ink Story):** Everyday-reality POV explainer about the ordinary WW2 serviceman of one army · thumbnail = one big cartoon soldier with exaggerated emotion + 1–3 contrarian words in yellow caps · title shape = "What Did [group] Actually Do All Day?" / "POV: You Are a [nation] Soldier" / "Your Life as Every Rank in [army]" · length = 16–20 min.

**Proof that the formula transfers:** Legacy of Steel (598 subs) swapped only the army (US → German) and got 150k (155x its median).
**Warning:** a search for "what did ww2 soldiers actually do all day" returns 15+ near-copies with 0–3,500 views ("Last Stand POV", "HistoryButSimple", "Explain Beyond"…). Copying the same subject with the same title gets nothing. Only a **new subject** worked.

## 3. Competitor #2 — Inside the Steel (structure reference)

- Channel: @InsideTheSteels, 13.5k subs, 36 videos, median 21k.
- Video: "Every Position Inside a 5-Man King Tiger Ranked From Safest to Deadliest" — https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NPBlovQ2rzk — 1.1M views, 22 min. Its position in the list puts it between 2026-09-20 and 2026-11-01, so it is **under 2 months old**.
- PROOF: **very fresh**. Two videos are over 1M, and the B-17 ranking (2026-09-20) has 281k.
- Thumbnails: an AI cutaway of one war machine with the crew visible in their seats. **No text.** Sky or field background. The click tactic is "see inside".
- Titles: "Every Position Inside a [N]-Man [vehicle] Ranked From Safest to Deadliest" / "What It Was Like to Fight Inside [position] at 25,000 Feet" / "How [N] Crewmen Ate, Slept & Used the Toilet…". The sub-niche is **one war machine and its crew**.
- Length: 16–30 min.

> **FORMULA CARD (Inside the Steel):** Seat-by-seat survival ranking inside one WW2 war machine · thumbnail = cutaway of the vehicle with the crew in their seats, no text · title shape = "Every Position Inside a [N]-Man [vehicle] Ranked From Safest to Deadliest" · length = 16–30 min.

**Status of that formula:** RED. Search returns 15+ copycats (destroyer, battleship, PT boat, Yamato, U-boat, submarine, carrier…) with 3 to 6,000 views. The Rising Guy copied "Every Position Inside a Tiger-I" and got **548 views**. That is the rule in action: a channel that owns a format leaves no room for a second copy.

### Structure of the King Tiger video (from the transcript, 3,831 words / 22:30)
| Time | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:37 | HOOK | "It looked like the safest place a soldier could sit… the five men inside knew every seat had its own way of killing you." The stake is a paradox (safe-looking ↔ deadly). No greeting. |
| 0:41–1:36 | Context | Henschel factory in Kassel, fewer than 500 built, crew layout. |
| 1:36–3:51 | Shared life | Noise, air, food, toilet, sleeping under the tank (the 70-ton-sinking danger). Strong human details. |
| 3:55–4:30 | Honest caveat | "Nobody kept a casualty table per seat… where evidence is thin, we'll say so." Builds trust. |
| 4:33 → 20:30 | Quests #5→#1 | Radio operator → driver → loader → gunner → commander. Each section ends with a bridge to the next man ("The man across the breech had no hatch at all"). |
| 14:14 | Mid-video CTA | "subscribe to Inside the Steel". |
| ~20:30–21:37 | Answer | How each seat got out of a burning tank. The commander is #1 because of the open hatch. |
| 21:37–22:30 | Recap | All five men again, with an emotional close. |

**Weak spots:**
- The #1 answer (commander) is predictable by the middle of the video, so the reveal loses power.
- Dead minutes: in the driver section, 9:01–10:46 drifts into fuel logistics and Kampfgruppe Peiper, which is not about the seat.
- The mid-video subscribe at 14:14 breaks the climb from #3 to #2.
- The recap repeats content, and at 22 min the video is long for the amount of new material.
- Some lists read like "…and… and…" (the radio operator's duties).

**Strengths to borrow (formula, not content):** a paradox hook, a shared-life block full of sensory detail, an honest note about the evidence, and bridges between sections.

**GAP (what the viewer wants next):** the same "what was it really like" lens for **other armies and ordinary men outside the famous machines**. Ink Story and Legacy of Steel cover US and German soldiers. Nobody has made a strong **Soviet** soldier day-in-the-life with this formula.
