# verify.md — facts to double-check before recording

Status: ✅ = confirmed by 2+ independent sources · ⚠️ = sources disagree, the script uses the safe wording · ❓ = single source, check it.
The script does not invent quotes. The "typical evening" mother is an illustrative scene, not a named real person, and the script frames it as "Picture a typical evening".

| # | Claim in script | Status | Source / note |
|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | London was bombed for 57 consecutive nights from 7 Sept 1940 | ✅ | IWM "The Blitz" pages; London Museum. One source says "56 of 57 days and nights": the standard figure is 57. |
| V2 | The government banned using the Tube as shelters; it had to keep transport running; fear of a "deep shelter mentality" | ⚠️ | TfL Archives (Google Arts & Culture): a 1924 directive ruled out stations as shelters. Spartacus Educational: the government ordered London Transport not to allow it. The words "deep shelter mentality" appear in histories (Calder, *The People's War*). Check the exact phrase if you want it on screen. |
| V3 | The cheapest ticket was 1½d; people bought it and refused to leave | ❓ | "Bought a ticket and refused to leave": TfL Archives ✅. The **1½d price** comes from popular histories and is not in the sources I checked. If you are unsure, change the line to "it cost them the price of the cheapest ticket" (and use thumbnail concept A, which doesn't depend on the price). |
| V4 | Crowds forced their way in at Liverpool Street in the first nights | ✅ | TfL Archives story ("crowds at Liverpool Street… tried to get in"). |
| V5 | "Within weeks" the government gave in | ⚠️ | TfL: by 21 Sept 1940, ~120,000 were sheltering with LPTB in control. Spartacus links the formal policy change to Herbert Morrison (October 1940). "Within weeks" covers both. |
| V6 | 177,000 slept underground on the night of 27 Sept 1940 | ✅ | London Museum (peak single night, 27 Sept 1940, ~5% of the population); Spartacus ("by the end of September"). |
| V7 | White lines: 8 ft from the edge until 7:30 pm; 4 ft from 8 pm to 10:30 pm; then trains stopped, lights dimmed, current cut | ❓ | Spartacus Educational quoting Angus Calder, *The People's War*. **This is the thumbnail's claim**, so it is worth one more check (London Transport Museum). |
| V8 | People slept on stairs, passages and stopped escalators | ✅ | Period photos (TfL Archives, IWM, London Museum: Bill Brandt / Henry Moore shelter images). |
| V9 | Queues from 10 a.m. | ⚠️ | Spartacus: "as early as ten in the morning"; Calder: "as early as six". The script uses 10 a.m. (the more cautious number). |
| V10 | Black-market platform places sold for up to 2s 6d | ❓ | Spartacus Educational. |
| V11 | Shelter tickets; ~10% of places left free for people caught in a raid | ❓ | Spartacus ("about 10 per cent… unallocated"); TfL 1943 Shelter Safety Code ("have your shelter ticket ready"). |
| V12 | Aldwych branch closed to trains and given to shelterers | ✅ | Spartacus; widely documented (Aldwych closed 22 Sept 1940). |
| V13 | 24,000 bunks; 27 miles end to end | ✅ | TfL Archives story (both figures); ~24,000 also in other sources. |
| V14 | Rationing of bacon, butter and sugar | ✅ | Started 8 Jan 1940 (bacon, butter, sugar): standard fact (IWM, National Archives). |
| V15 | Tube Refreshment Specials left depots every afternoon; ~7 tons of food a night; ~30,000 buns; 134 canteen points (Dec 1940) | ⚠️ | Dec 1940 newspaper reports (Papers Past, NZ, reprinting London reports): "six depots… seven tons of food… 30,000 buns", "134 canteen points". Londonist confirms 7 tons. Tea volumes differ between sources, so the script avoids a tea number per night. |
| V16 | Over half a million gallons of tea by the end of the war | ❓ | London Transport Museum estimate, ~545,454 gallons (via search summary). Check on ltmuseum.co.uk. |
| V17 | Holborn: capacity 4,600, 4 Elsan toilets + 4 buckets | ❓ | TfL Archives story (Google Arts & Culture). |
| V18 | Lice; fear of epidemics | ✅ | Widely documented (Horder Committee on shelter health, autumn 1940). |
| V19 | Mosquitoes; spraying by Feb 1941, paused while people slept | ✅ | TfL Archives story (Feb 1941 note about sprays and compressors). Shelterers' bite complaints: Wikipedia "London Underground mosquito". The script does NOT repeat the "new species evolved in the Tube" myth, which DNA studies have debunked. |
| V20 | First aid posts and nurses at the big shelters | ❓ | Common in histories (medical aid posts at large stations). A source is needed for exact numbers; the script gives none. |
| V21 | ~25,000 children a night at the peak | ❓ | TfL Archives story ("an estimated 25,000 children"). |
| V22 | Libraries, concerts, films, lectures | ✅ | Spartacus (Westminster Library gave 2,000 books; ENSA concerts and films; lectures); TfL Archives (gramophone records, parties). |
| V23 | ~63 million shelter visits over the war | ❓ | TfL Archives story: "around 63,000,000 people sheltered" (total visits, not unique people; the script says "visits"). |
| V24 | Marble Arch subway hit, 17 Sept 1940, 20 killed | ❓ | Spartacus. |
| V25 | Trafalgar Square and Bounds Green hit in October 1940 | ⚠️ | TfL: Trafalgar Sq 12 Oct (7 killed), Bounds Green the next night (19). Spartacus gives 7 Oct / 8 Oct. The script says only "in October". |
| V26 | Balham, 14 Oct 1940: bus fell into the crater; water and sewage flooded the tunnel; 68 died, 4 of them staff | ⚠️ | Death toll 64–68 depending on the source (TfL: 64; Wikipedia list: 68 = 64 public + 4 staff; one source dates it to 15 Oct). If you prefer caution, change it to "More than sixty people died that night." |
| V27 | Bank, 11 Jan 1941: bomb hit the booking hall; "more than fifty" killed; army built a bridge over the crater | ⚠️ | Toll 53–56 (TfL 53; Wikipedia 56; one outlier says 111). "More than fifty" is safe. The bridge (Royal Engineers' Bailey-type bridge across the crater) is widely reported; check one source. |
| V28 | Deep-level shelters: 8 built, ready only after the Blitz | ✅ | London Transport Museum "Deep-level shelters" (8 of 10 planned, opened 1942). |
| V29 | Nov 1940 count: 4% Tube, 9% public shelters, 27% domestic shelters, ~60% at home | ✅ | London Museum (4% + 9%); Spartacus; johndclare.net (Longmate); Calder. One caveat: the 27% may come from a wider survey than the public-shelter census. The script says "officials counted". |
| V30 | Bethnal Green, 3 March 1943: a new rocket battery fired nearby, a woman fell, 173 died, no bomb hit | ✅ | The National Archives (official inquiry); History in Focus (IHR); Wikipedia. Victoria Park rocket battery: inquiry report. |
| V31 | The Bethnal Green report was kept quiet until near the end of the war | ⚠️ | The Dunne inquiry report was withheld and published in 1945 (National Archives). "Until the war was nearly over" is safe; check the month if you want to be exact. |
| V32 | Last night, 6 May 1945: 344 shelterers | ❓ | TfL Archives story. |
