## Option 1 — "The Line Behind One Soldier" ⭐ RECOMMENDED
- **Premise:** One American rifleman, Normandy 1944. We trace his food, ships, fuel, mail and medical care back through everyone who made them possible. Then we answer with a number: how many men in uniform stood behind each man who actually walked toward the enemy.
- **Formula:** NEXT EPISODE (Ink Story's outlier "What Did WW2 Soldiers Actually Do All Day?" → "Who kept him there, and how many of them?").
- **Why people click:** It directly answers the open question the ×32 outlier left in 875K viewers' heads. It's a "numbers that shock" angle (1 : 7 in Europe, about 1 : 18 Army-wide). And it matches our strengths: logistics, costs, everyday life, simple maps.
- **Why it is NOT a copy:** Different question (the system, not the soldier's schedule), different structure (a supply chain from the US factory to the foxhole, not a clock), a different answer (a ratio derived from official Army figures), and different content (Liberty ships, Red Ball Express, V-mail, division slice, the 1944 riflemen shortage). Their title formula and timeline are not reused.

## Option 2 — "Britain Paid for WW2 Until 2006"
- **Premise:** Britain made its last loan payment to the US and Canada for WW2-era loans at the end of 2006 [VERIFY]. How did a winner end up paying for 61 years?
- **Formula:** FORMULA TRANSFER. The Dictator Lab's ×581 money paradox ("How did Germany pay?") applied to a winner, Britain.
- **Why people click:** A shocking date in the title, money, and "winners pay too" goes against expectations.
- **Why it is NOT a copy:** Different country, different mechanism (Lend-Lease end, 1946 Anglo-American loan), the opposite story arc (victor in debt).
- Risk: economics is harder to animate. Needs careful verification of loan figures.

## Option 3 — "What Japan Forgot to Bomb at Pearl Harbor" (85th anniversary)
- **Premise:** On 7 December 1941 Japan hit the battleships but left the fuel tank farm, repair yards and submarine base. Why that mattered more than the ships.
- **Formula:** TREND × WWII (85th anniversary on 7 Dec 2026, 26 days away) + FORMAT SWITCH (usually told with archive footage, here as a whiteboard map).
- **Why people click:** Anniversary search spike, "what they missed" curiosity gap.
- **Why it is NOT a copy:** The angle is widely discussed, so the risk of looking like a copy is higher than Options 1–2. We would need a fresh structure (the "second strike that never came" as a game).
- Deadline: would have to publish before ~5 Dec 2026.

## Recommendation
**Option 1.** It has the strongest and freshest evidence (an outlier at 16 days that is still climbing), our format gives a clear advantage (maps, counters, chains), and the answer is a number we can derive from official sources. **Option 3** is the best candidate for video #2 because of the anniversary date.
