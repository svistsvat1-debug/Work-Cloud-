## Description

A motor keeps spinning, but the last gear it drives is set in solid concrete. So why doesn't anything break?

The trick is gear reduction. Between the motor and the concrete are twelve pairs of gears, and each pair slows the spin 50 times. Those slowdowns multiply: 50 × 50 × 50… twelve times = 244,140,625,000,000,000,000.

With the motor at about 200 rpm, the last gear would need over two trillion years to turn once. That's more than a hundred times the age of the universe (about 13.8 billion years). To the concrete, it's basically standing still.

The real machine is "Machine with Concrete", a kinetic sculpture by artist Arthur Ganson. This cartoon is a simplified version of the idea.
